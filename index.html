Terpene color · our own classification · Sep 27, 2026

Four terpene-led groups, read straight from the COA.

Every flower lot gets one of four groups from its lab terpene panel, plus an optional note, with a six-bar chart of its chemistry. It is plain arithmetic, so the same COA always gives the same answer. The names describe chemistry and aroma, not effects. Effects will be learned from customer ratings.

The algorithm

How a COA becomes a group

Inputs are the lab's % by mass for each terpene and the total terpenes. Each family's share is its % of total terpenes. Checked in this order; the first match wins:

  1. Check the panel. Empty, non-numeric, negative or impossible values (a share over 100%, or shares summing well over 100%) → Insufficient data. Fractions (0–1) are converted to percent. Aliases like "beta-myrcene" are summed, not dropped.
  2. Terpinolene-led if terpinolene is at least 10% of terpenes.
  3. Myrcene-led if β-myrcene is at least 10% and more than half of both the β-caryophyllene family and limonene + linalool. A clearly pinene-dominant lot also lands here, with a pinene note.
  4. Limonene/Linalool-led if limonene + linalool is at least 10% and ahead of β-caryophyllene + α-humulene.
  5. Caryophyllene-led if β-caryophyllene + α-humulene is at least 10% and at least as large as limonene + linalool.
  6. Nothing reaches 10% → Insufficient data (never a guessed group).

Optional notes (shown only when present)

A note flags a second family that stands clearly above its usual level: when α+β-pinene is notable, when linalool clearly leads limonene, and for citrus in a Myrcene-led lot. Most lots have no note. That's by design: a note is information, not a sub-category.

Confidence chip

We re-run each lot 1,000 times with every lab value nudged by up to ±10% (normal lab variation). High means the same group ≥95% of the time, Medium ≥80%, and Low below that, a lot sitting on a boundary.

The four groups

One symbol per group

Why four: published clustering finds 3 broad bundles in 42,843 US samples (Smith 2022). Our own 242 panels split most reproducibly into 4 (bootstrap agreement 0.98 vs 0.79 for six). Pinene and linalool never form their own groups in our data, so they are notes, not groups.

Two rule choices for David

  1. Myrcene beats a bigger β-caryophyllene. A lot is Myrcene-led when β-myrcene is at least half of each other family, even if β-caryophyllene is larger. β-caryophyllene is the biggest terpene in most lots, so requiring myrcene to win outright would make Myrcene-led rare. Keep it?
  2. Pinene-dominant lots go under Myrcene-led, with a pinene note (4 today). Keep it, or add a fifth "Pinene-led" group if they become common?
What a label looks like

Group, note, confidence, and the chemistry behind it

The six-bar chart shows each family's share of total terpenes. It's the evidence behind the group, not a second category. The text under each bar gives the leading family's actual share and how it compares with our catalog.

How the rule was chosen

A Grok 4.7 swarm, scored by one fixed test

Three Grok 4.7 (xhigh) designers each took a different approach, a Grok judge compared them, a Grok red-team attacked the winner, and a final Grok pass hardened the chosen rule. Claude agents only ran the code through the same scoring harness: stability under ±10% lab noise, agreement with the natural four-cluster split, and balance.

What the science says

Why the names aren't effect claims

Terpenes

  • Controlled human studies exist for D-limonene and α-pinene (both with THC) and β-myrcene (alone). β-caryophyllene and linalool have a small odor study with an odorless control group, and linalool's anxiety change there was not significant. Terpinolene has only an uncontrolled EEG study.
  • The one positive cannabis-relevant result: a high 15 mg dose of pure limonene reduced some THC-induced anxiety. α-pinene did not protect memory. A pure myrcene capsule impaired some driving measures; that is not a sleep effect.
  • Receptor results are mixed (newer frog-egg cell studies show weak activity); relevance to smoking flower is unresolved.
  • Terpene numbers only loosely predict how flower smells (a trained sensory panel, 91 samples).

THC, CBD, indica/sativa

  • THC drives intensity. Label THC % is not delivered dose: experienced users partly self-titrate (JAMA Psychiatry 2020; Scientific Reports 2021).
  • CBD can soften, do nothing, or even intensify THC, depending on dose and ratio.
  • Retail indica/sativa labels are unreliable product identities; same-name products differ genetically and chemically.

What we can say to customers

  1. Our groups describe the lab-measured terpene chemistry and aroma family, not how it will make you feel.
  2. THC drives intensity, but the % on the label isn't the dose you get.
  3. Everyone responds differently: your ratings, and our customers' ratings, are the best guide.

Left out on purpose (Sep 27): parked, not forbidden

  • Feeling words per group ("relaxing", "uplifting"): no controlled evidence at flower doses. They come back as measured profiles once a group has ≥20 customer ratings.
  • Terpinolene = energizing, myrcene = couch-lock, linalool = calm: uncontrolled, not significant, or contradicted, as listed above.
  • Indica / sativa / hybrid: unreliable as a product identity.
  • THC % as a strength ranking: not the dose people actually take in. Show it, but don't rank on it.
  • Entourage-effect and receptor-mechanism stories: the lab results are mixed and not tied to smoking flower.
  • Minor terpenes (ocimene, bisabolol, nerolidol, guaiol and others): not measured on every COA, so the groups use the six core families only.
  • Pinene-led as a fifth group (open question): today pinene-dominant lots (pinene ≥15% of terpenes and ≥3 points ahead of every other family) fall under Myrcene-led with an Earth note. That is 6 of 264 rows, 4 strains: Blue Dream (pinene 44%), Late Night Hype (40%), Lemon Cherry Gelato (37%, myrcene only 6%) and Stellar Days (26%). A fifth Earth group would describe them honestly (pine, fresh, herbal), but it is small, so its ratings profile will take longest to reach 20.
  • Six sub-profiles: dropped because they repeated the four groups.

Verdict

  • Keep the four groups.
  • Pinene stays a note, not a fifth group. Pinene-heavy flower sits in a pinene+myrcene zone: the high-pinene cluster averaged 26% pinene and 32% myrcene, and a 2025 140-flower study shows the same. Only about 4 of 242 distinct panels would qualify. Revisit with more COAs.

  • "Our groups describe lab-measured chemistry and aroma, not how it will make you feel."

  • Every rating display: "Customer reports, not a guaranteed effect."
  • Never show effect words before someone rates (expectation changes ratings).

Public group profile—≥50 customers, ≥5 lots, no lot over 35%, interval ±0.75
"Established in our data"—≥100 customers, ≥10 lots, replicated in a later period
Ranking products or groups against each other—Never below 100 unique customers; above that only with interval ±0.75, overlapping route/dose, several lots, and a pooled model (not raw averages)

Why: with a typical spread of 2.5 points, 20 ratings give ±1.1 points of uncertainty, 50 give ±0.7 and 100 give ±0.5.

Method gaps to fix (valid)

  • "Not detected", "below the lab's limit" and "not tested" all count as zero today.
  • COA test date, analyte panel and lab method aren't recorded.
  • Total terpene amount is ignored; only proportions count.
  • The ±10% noise behind the confidence chips is assumed, not measured.
  • Duplicate panels inflate row counts; validate on distinct tests.
  • Percentages are compositional, and 4 vs 5 groups depends on how you represent them.
  • Myrcene-led can win at half the size of another family (open decision).

Checked and corrected

  • Hermes re-quoted an old "9 of 11 held-out" figure. It was retracted on Sep 26 (mostly in-sample) and measured the old outside color labels, so it doesn't apply.
  • Hermes' example pinene strains didn't match our rows (a parsing slip). Ours are Blue Dream, Late Night Hype, Lemon Cherry Gelato and Stellar Days; the count agrees.
  • Hermes corrected its own summary of the CBD-terpene sleep trial (PMID 39167421): 300 mg CBD with 1 mg each of eight terpenes, not 15 mg + 75 mg. The table uses the corrected version.
  • "Explanations don't match the code": the check order guarantees the stated comparisons, so it isn't a bug, though the code should spell them out.

What's left to make this real

Build

  1. Port the rule into the app (PHP, same logic, epsilon-safe; Grok's porting notes are saved) with the Python version as the test oracle.
  2. COA intake checks: the COA total must match the lot, flag mg/g vs %, and reject one terpene test linked to different strains.
  3. Shelf tags and product pages: group symbol, optional note, the six-bar chart, and "not an effect claim".

  1. Collect ratings: live in customer order history. v2 is the default and v1 is switchable (Sep 28). Next: a QR code on the tag and the post-pickup email link.
