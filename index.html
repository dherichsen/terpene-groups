Terpene families · our own classification · Nov 10, 2026

Five terpene families, read straight from the COA.

Every flower lot gets one of five families from its lab terpene panel, each with its own colour and symbol, plus an optional myrcene note and a six-bar chart of its chemistry. Five ordered tests decide it, so the same COA always gives the same answer. The families follow the chemical types that published studies of lab-tested flower report; the cut-off numbers are our own. Colours and symbols say which family a lot is in, not how it will feel.

The algorithm

How a COA becomes a family

Inputs are the lab's % by weight for each terpene and the total terpenes. Checked in this order; the first match wins:

  1. Check the panel. No usable total, a non-numeric or negative value, one terpene larger than the total, or limonene, linalool and β-caryophyllene all missing → Insufficient data (never a guessed family).
  2. Coral · Terpinolene-led if terpinolene is at least 15% of total terpenes.
  3. Orange · Pinene-forward if α-pinene + β-pinene is at least 9% of total terpenes and at least three-quarters of the limonene.
  4. Deep blue · Caryophyllene-led if limonene is 0.30% by weight or less. With that little limonene, β-caryophyllene is usually the largest terpene.
  5. Green · Linalool-forward if linalool is at least two-thirds of the limonene.
  6. Gold · Limonene-forward for everything else: limonene above 0.30% with linalool well behind it.

Optional note (shown only when present)

appears when β-myrcene is the largest single terpene and at least 20% of total terpenes. Myrcene tops lots in four of the five families, so it works as a note on top of the family, not a family of its own. Most lots have no note.

Confidence chip

We re-run each lot 1,000 times with every lab value nudged by up to ±10% (normal lab variation). High means the same family at least 95% of the time, Medium at least 80%, and Low below that. A lot that is not High also shows the family it borders.

The five families

One colour and one symbol per family

The set runs hot to cool, from full sun to crescent moon. Each symbol also reads in black and white, so a tag works without colour. Under each colour is the old shelf colour and name it replaces. Use the dropdown on a card to try another symbol for that family. Your pick shows across this page and stays on this device.

At tag size, colour and grayscale:

Why five: studies that sort lab-tested flower by terpenes keep finding a terpinolene type, a myrcene and pinene type, and a β-caryophyllene and limonene type (Smith 2022; Fischedick 2017). Coral and Orange are the first two. The third holds 208 of our 242 lab panels, so we split it three ways by limonene and linalool: Deep blue, Green and Gold.

What a label looks like

Family, note, confidence, and the chemistry behind it

The six-bar chart shows each family's share of total terpenes. It's the evidence behind the family, not a second category. The line under each bar is the exact test the lot passed, with the numbers that were checked.

The studies behind each family

The published support for each family

Five studies sort lab-tested flower by its terpenes, from 19 samples to 42,843. Each family below lists the strongest published support for it. Breeders have also produced chemovars rich in single terpenes, "for example, limonene, pinene, terpinolene, and linalool" (Lewis 2018).

Also consistent: six clusters in 140 flowers on the German market (Herwig 2025). We searched PubMed and Europe PMC on Nov 10, 2026 for a published cut-off number for placing a single lot by its terpenes and found none, so the five lines are ours, set on our 242 distinct lab panels.

How many familiesThe two largest datasets each find three clusters (Smith 2022; Reimann-Philipp 2020). The smaller studies find five or six.Five is our shelf choice. The studies back the terpenes we split on but not the number of families.

Strain namesPlants grown under one cultivar name varied widely in terpene profile (Booth 2020). In Nevada, 396 strain names covered three chemical clusters (Reimann-Philipp 2020). Sativa and Indica labels tracked a few terpenes, not overall genetics (Watts 2021).Each lot is sorted from its own COA. The name is never used.
